# Confidentiality check (run on every draft before showing it)

A public profile is read by recruiters and by the owner's current and former employers, clients, patients, students
and competitors. The test is simple: **would the employer, or the people the work was done for, be comfortable seeing
this sentence on a public page?** If you're unsure, generalize it or leave it out, and mention it to the owner.

## Remove or generalize

| Never publish | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Internal system names, hostnames, server/database/table names, internal tool codenames | the kind of system ("the company's billing platform") |
| Incidents, outages, breaches, rollbacks, audits, lawsuits, complaints | the capability ("hardened the release process") |
| Customer, patient, student, client or case names or details; any personal data | the population ("for 200+ students", only if verified and not identifying) |
| Rare facts that identify someone even without a name (a national award + year + school; a small team + a specific event) | a broader version ("coached students to national olympiad medals") |
| Vendor, partner or supplier names, unless the employer has announced the partnership publicly | "a third-party payments provider" |
| Commercial terms: prices, margins, revenue, contract values, budgets, headcount plans | leave out, or use a public figure only |
| Numbers that look bad ("fixed 40 defects in my own release", "cut churn from 30%") | the positive outcome, if verified |
| Unreleased products, internal project codenames, roadmap | only products listed publicly (company website, app store, press) |
| Work someone else built or led | "worked with", "contributed to", or leave it out |
| Anything under an NDA, or confidential by professional rules (clinical, legal, financial, HR) | leave out |

## Private sources

Appraisals, internal reports and emails can serve as *evidence* in the ledger, because they prove a claim to you. Never
quote them, name them or reveal their numbers publicly. Employer-owned performance data (exam results, sales
figures, ratings, defect counts) stays off the profile unless the employer has published it, even when it's good.
For a result that fell short of its goal, publish neither number. Describe the work instead.

## Check before showing

- [ ] Every product or project named is on the employer's **public** website, app store or press release. Check if
      unsure, and record the URL in the evidence ledger.
- [ ] The owner did, or clearly co-led, every achievement. Team results are written as team results.
- [ ] Every number is in the evidence ledger as *Verified* or *Owner-confirmed*, and it is a result, not a target.
- [ ] Nothing would let a reader identify a specific customer, patient, student or colleague.
- [ ] Personal side projects use personal accounts (personal email, personal GitHub/portfolio), not employer ones, and
      contain no employer material.

Show the owner any line you generalized and explain why in one short sentence, so they can disagree.
