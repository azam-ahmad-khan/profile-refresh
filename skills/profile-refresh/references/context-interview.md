# Context interview

Most people don't keep notes on their own work. Often the resume is all there is. This step gets the rest out of
the owner's head (and, if they choose, out of their work tools) before anything is drafted.

## 1. How much evidence is there?

Ask once: "Besides your resume, do you have anything that shows your work, such as appraisals, certificates,
portfolio pieces, published work, project documents or awards?" Then pick a route:

| Evidence | Route |
|---|---|
| Rich (documents, tickets, portfolio) | Read them, then do a short interview to fill gaps |
| Some | Read what exists, do the interview, and optionally the extraction prompt (§4) |
| Resume only | The interview (§3) is the main source. Offer the extraction prompt (§4) |

## 2. Which way should the profile lean?

Ask before drafting, because it changes every section: "Should the profile show you as you are today, or lean toward
where you want to go next?" Offer the owner's own options, for example:

- **Deeper in the current specialty** (a senior or expert version of today's role)
- **Toward a new direction** within reach: AI-leaning, management or leadership, consulting, teaching or training,
  product, a different industry
- **Same role, different setting** (another country, sector or company size)

Ask for 2–3 real job postings they'd like to get (links or pasted text). They drive keywords, the gap analysis and
the proof-of-work idea. If they have none, offer to find a few and let them pick.

For a pivot, ask: "What have you already done that points that way, even in a small way?" Side projects, tools tried,
courses, training others and volunteering all count. Those are the bridges. The profile leads with them, and missing
bridges become the proof-of-work project.

## 3. Interview (one or two questions at a time)

Story questions bring out more than "list your achievements":

1. Walk me through a normal week. What takes most of your time?
2. What do people come to you for, the thing you're the go-to person for?
3. Tell me about a problem you solved that you're proud of. What was the situation, what did you do, and what changed?
4. What is different at work because you were there (a process, a tool, a team, a result)?
5. Who do you serve and how many (customers, patients, students, colleagues, budget, region)? Ballpark is fine;
   we'll mark it as unconfirmed.
6. Which tools, methods or certifications do you use that *aren't* on your resume yet?
7. What feedback or recognition have you had (reviews, awards, thank-you notes, promotions)?
8. What work would you happily never do again? (This tells us what *not* to emphasize.)

For each answer, record the claim in the evidence ledger as *Owner-confirmed*, or *Open* if it's vague or has a number
with no source, and ask a follow-up only where a fact or a source is missing.

## 4. Work-extraction prompt (optional)

If the owner uses an AI assistant **that their employer approves** and that can see their work (email, documents,
tickets, chat), offer this prompt for them to run *there*. Tell them plainly: check the company's AI and data policy
first. You will never see their work systems, only the summary they choose to paste back. Fill in the angle brackets
before giving it to them.

```text
I'm updating my resume and LinkedIn profile. Using only work I personally did or co-led between <start> and <today>,
build an achievement inventory from what you can see (my email, documents, tickets, meetings, chats).

Rules:
- Generalize everything confidential. No internal system or project codenames, no customer, patient, student or
  client names, no vendor or partner names unless publicly announced, no prices, revenue, budgets or contract terms,
  no incidents or defect counts, no colleague names.
- Separate results from goals or plans. Mark anything that was a target, not an outcome.
- Separate what I did myself from what my team did.
- Only include numbers you can point to, and say what kind of source each comes from (e.g. "quarterly report",
  "ticket history"), without the internal name or link.

Output a table: Period | What I did (action) | Outcome (verified / unverified) | Skills shown | Source type.
Then list 5 themes that recur across my work, with a lean toward <target direction, e.g. AI, leadership>.
```

When they paste the result back: run the [confidentiality check](confidentiality.md) on it anyway, add each line to
the ledger as *Owner-confirmed* (not *Verified*, since you haven't seen the source), and ask about anything that looks
like a goal or a team result.

If they don't use such an assistant: suggest a 15-minute look through their sent email, calendar and files for the
last 12 months, jotting one line per notable piece of work. Then come back to the interview.
