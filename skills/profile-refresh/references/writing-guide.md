# Writing guide (any profession)

Write in the owner's voice and their field's vocabulary. Ask for one or two examples of writing they like (their own
or a peer's) if you are unsure of tone. Draft from the evidence ledger only.

## Evidence statuses

| Status | Meaning | Can publish? |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified** | Backed by something other than the owner's own resume or profile: an appraisal, certificate, published work, public page, official letter (record the source) | Yes |
| **Owner-confirmed** | The owner states it (including everything that's only on their resume or current profile) and confirms it when asked | Yes, without numbers unless they confirm the exact number and where it comes from |
| **Open** | Vague, unproven, a goal or target, or contradicts another source | No. Ask about it |

Watch for: goals written as results ("will reduce…"), team results claimed as personal ones, inflated titles,
date gaps or overlaps between resume and LinkedIn, and numbers with no source.

## Sections

**Quiet or open search?** Everyone signed in to LinkedIn, colleagues included, sees the headline, About and
experience. Public-profile settings only hide them from people who aren't signed in. So if the employer must not know
(a *quiet* search), lean through skills and achievements, not announcements: no "aspiring X", "→ X" or "open to
roles". Use those only in an *open* search, where the owner is fine with the employer knowing.

**Headline (≤220 chars).** Current or target role | 2–3 specialties recruiters search for | optional proof point or
domain. Put the most important words first. Example shapes:
- `Registered Nurse | Critical care & patient education | Pursuing clinical informatics`
- Quiet pivot (accountant → data analyst): `Senior Accountant | Reporting automation with Power BI & SQL | Month-end close`
- Open pivot: `Senior Accountant → Data Analyst | Power BI, SQL, financial reporting`

**Location.** City and country the owner wants to be hired in. If they are relocating, ask whether to show the target
city and confirm it matches what they will say in interviews. Remote or hybrid preference goes in Open to Work's
workplace options, not in the location field. Add it to About only in an open search.

**About (≤2,600 chars).** 3–5 short paragraphs: (1) who you are and what you do, in one or two sentences; (2) 2–3
verified highlights; (3) how you work or what you care about; (4) what you're looking for, only in an open
search; (5) optional "Skills:" line with keywords. The first ~3 lines show before "see more", so make them count.

**Experience (≤2,000 chars per role).** One line of context (team or scope, without confidential detail), then 3–5
bullets: action verb + what + verified outcome. Keep the most recent role richest and shorten older ones. Titles must
match the employer's official title (ask the owner to check their contract, offer letter or HR system). Mention a
more descriptive title only in the description ("Role: …").

**Skills.** Up to 5 can be pinned as top skills. Pick ones that match the target roles and are backed by evidence.

**Open to Work titles.** Up to 5. Choose titles a recruiter would search for, not internal ones, and check the
canonical names (see [linkedin-gotchas.md](linkedin-gotchas.md)).

**Resume.** Same facts and titles as LinkedIn, tighter wording, usually 1–2 pages. Keep the owner's format. Save a
new dated copy. If career-ops is in use, update `cv.md` only with the owner's OK (see [career-ops.md](career-ops.md)).

## Style

- Plain, specific and active. Cut "passionate", "results-driven" and "synergy".
- Keywords from real job ads for the target roles. Ask the owner for 2–3 postings they like, or search for them.
- No emoji unless the owner wants them. First person is fine on LinkedIn.
